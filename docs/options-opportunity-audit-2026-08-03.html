The app found signals. It did not yet capture the trade.

Market produced one verifiable 4.1× NVDA call setup and several 3–5× NVDA put setups during the recent AI sell-off. But zero option alerts and zero paper orders followed. The Google rebound is not reconstructable from Market's retained chain data, and a 5.27× TSLA put was missed entirely.

Audit date: 3 August 2026 · Point-in-time window: 13 July–3 August 2026 · System: live PostgreSQL professional-v3 path · Scope: GOOGL, TSLA, INTC, MU, SNDK, NVDA and related semiconductor volatility

D+

Partial discovery; failed operational capture

The system deserves credit for surfacing a real, executable-looking NVDA 4× setup on the primary radar. It does not deserve credit for a captured trade: there was no alert, paper order, governed entry, or exit. Across the user's named stocks, the evidence is sparse enough that most “misses” cannot even be audited contract by contract.

Investment conclusion The opportunity class is real enough to build for, but the current product is a scanner—not yet a repeatable short-horizon options strategy.

What worked: broad ticker coverage, honest execution blockers, one primary-surface NVDA win, several secondary NVDA setups.

What failed: intraday coverage, action delivery, exact 3×/4× measurement, contract retention, executable exit accounting, and true missed-winner recall.

Why the current system falls short

01

Cadence misses the setup

Broad chains run premarket and around 09:40. QQQ/NVDA alone receive recurring full history. A midday capitulation or reversal in GOOGL, TSLA, INTC, MU or SNDK can vanish between snapshots.

02

The ranker does not model rebounds

Professional v3 is 65% liquidity and 35% convexity. It has no sell-off depth, exhaustion, reclaim, relative strength, catalyst, IV-regime or flow features.

03

3× and 4× do not exist

Outcomes, EV and UI track 2×/5×/10×. Missed-winner learning starts at 5× gross, so a 4× trade is invisible to the learning objective.

04

“Peak” is not an exit

The PostgreSQL learning query uses peak return. Generic outcomes mark midpoint-to-midpoint, and some shadow entries predate the decision timestamp. Existing hit rates therefore do not demonstrate realizable P&L.

05

The denominator is missing

Broad raw quotes retain seven days and most rejected contracts are discarded. “Missed winners” are drawn from selected decisions, not every scanned contract, so recall cannot be measured honestly.

06

The action layer is disconnected

The audited basket has 2,725 shadow rows but zero trade alerts and zero paper orders. Paper actions are disabled and risk-sleeve capital is unset; legacy alert/attribution code is not called by the production refresh.

Build toward short-term 3×/4×—without turning it into lottery hunting

The correct objective is not “find contracts that can mathematically 4×.” It is “find a repeatable, liquid, time-bounded setup whose executable return distribution supports a 3×/4× exit ladder after losses and costs.”

caught_3x_or_4x = timely_signal × exact_contract × executable_entry × governed_exit × retained_evidence
P0 · Tape
Capture the opportunity before optimizing the model.
Add adaptive 5–15 minute chains for a small volatile basket—GOOGL, TSLA, INTC, MU, SNDK, NVDA, QQQ/SOXX—and retain a compact 21–30 day audit tape for every scanned contract and reject.
Unlocks honest point-in-time replay and same-contract path reconstruction without full-chain storage for all 80 names.
P0 · Labels
Add executable 2×/3×/4× outcomes.
Enter at first post-signal ask plus slippage; exit at first attainable bid or governed limit. Record time-to-target, MFE, MAE, giveback, expiry risk and missed fill. Keep 10× as a tail diagnostic.
Prevents midpoint peaks and hindsight exits from masquerading as captured returns.
P1 · Setup
Create a dedicated sell-off/rebound family.
Test roughly 7–60 DTE, moderate-delta liquid calls and call spreads across several expiries. Condition on drawdown depth, capitulation/exhaustion, reclaim confirmation, relative strength, catalyst integrity, IV and spread quality.
Targets the user's actual opportunity instead of generic LEAP/lottery convexity.
P1 · Recall
Replay the full denominator.
For each timestamp, compare all scanned contracts with surfaced, alerted, actionable, filled and exited subsets. Report precision@K, 3×/4× recall, signal delay, net expectancy, concentration and opportunity cost.
Distinguishes a model miss from a data miss, publication miss or execution miss.
P1 · Action
Connect v3 to paper alerts and staged exits.
Every alert needs symbol, exact OCC contract, limit, size, invalidation, TTL and exit ladder: de-risk near 2×, harvest at 3×/4× or trail with explicit rules. Keep execution paper-only until calibrated.
Converts a table row into an auditable trading decision.
P2 · Learn
Repair the learning owner.
Use executable realized exits, not peak return; add regime/DTE/delta/IV cohorts with shrinkage; reject unsupported proposal parameters; reintroduce true attribution and alert feedback in the PostgreSQL path.
Makes the system improve from outcomes it could actually have traded.

Evidence and limitations

  • PostgreSQL: raw option snapshots/quotes, analysis decisions/outcomes/strategy evaluations, option-radar publications, alerts and paper orders. All historical calculations in this report use locally retained point-in-time rows.
  • Code owners: jobs/refresh_options_radar.py, database/options_analysis.py, database/outcomes.py, database/strategy_learning.py, database/retention.py, database/options_retention.py, and config.yaml.
  • Regime sources: AP: rotation away from AI winners; Reuters: chip sell-off and options activity; Reuters: MU/SNDK weakness; Tesla Q2 release; Micron Q3 release.
  • Historical-chain limitation: the exact GOOGL 4× option cannot be reconstructed from Market's retained data. No exact contract claim is made without broker/vendor NBBO history.
  • Execution caveat: “first bid reaches 3×/4×” is a conservative-side mark, but not proof that the full desired size filled at that bid. Future replay must model available size, slippage and fees.
